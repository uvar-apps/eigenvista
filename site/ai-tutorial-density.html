07.2 / EIGENVISTA FIELD MANUAL

Look inside electron density.

EigenVista 0.10.3 groups manual controls in four Tools tabs: Molecule, Fields, Surfaces and Appearance. You can watch or adjust the scene while your agent works; scientific requests do not depend on which tab is open. The current 0.10.3 package includes the same scientific workflows. See the current workspace.

Move from a signed orbital to a density envelope. Explore the sampled field first, then make geometry you can shade, cut open and save.

Use the same real calculation

Complete AI connection setup and read the external-agent guide. This lesson uses the connection tools included in the EigenVista 0.10.3 Mac app. Give the prompts to your external Codex, Claude Code, Pi, OpenCode or other local agent; there is no embedded EigenVista chat box.

Use the included bicyclic-c7h10.molden, the same 17-atom C7H10 example as the recorded orbital lesson. Your agent can also download it into the input folder. Use the connected window and folders chosen during setup. Keep the original input and use new filenames for your results.

Here density is the occupation-weighted sum of squared listed orbitals: ρ(r) = Σ ni|ψi(r)|². It is nonnegative and has units of electrons/bohr³. It has no positive/negative orbital phase. The importer cannot recover omitted orbitals or core electrons, and the source does not identify its calculation method. This is an occupation-derived density, not a new SCF calculation or an independently supplied correlated density.

1. Discover the density by its meaning

Use our connected EigenVista window. Save any unsaved work to a new .eigenvista file in my output folder before opening another document; do not discard it. Open bicyclic-c7h10.molden from my examples folder. Find the total electron density derived from the listed orbital occupations, checking its scientific meaning rather than just its name. Tell me its units and the atom, basis-function and orbital counts. Remove any previous X-ray or clipping effect.

Checkpoint: the document has 17 atoms, 125 basis functions and 118 orbitals. The density is described as occupation-derived, with units of electrons/bohr³. Its label may simply say “Occupation-derived density (listed orbitals)”.

An imported cube with unspecified scalar meaning is unsuitable for this lesson: its filename cannot establish that it contains electron density.

2. Explore a volume without generating meshes

Show that total density as a volume preview at grid resolution 48 and isovalue 0.04 electrons/bohr³. Use #93e8d9 for the density, 100% opacity and a #17232d background. Show the molecule as ball and stick. Wait until the preview finishes, then fit it in the view. Confirm that no surface meshes were created. Export density-volume-48.png in my output folder and show me the image.

Checkpoint: a volume preview appears at 48³ and no saved surfaces have been created. The density is sampled directly for rendering. At this stage an image exists, but there is no new mesh geometry to save.

The 0.04 electrons/bohr³ value controls how the field is displayed. Volume opacity accumulates samples along viewing rays; the result is not a sharp physical skin around a molecule.

3. Compare a sampled raycast

Remember the current viewing angle and framing. Keep the same density, 0.04 isovalue and colors, but switch to a sampled raycast preview at grid resolution 64. Wait until it finishes, then restore the same angle and framing for comparison. Confirm that there are still no saved surface meshes. Export density-raycast-64.png in my output folder, show it beside the volume image and explain what changed.

Checkpoint: the raycast preview uses a 64³ grid and the saved-surface count stays zero. The apparent boundary is rendered from sampled values; neither preview creates mesh geometry. Because this step also increases spatial resolution, use the same resolution for both modes if you want to compare only the rendering method.

Sampling quality is a separate display choice: it changes samples along rays, while grid resolution changes samples in space. Neither creates additional quantum-chemistry input data.

4. Keep a density surface

Remember the current viewing angle and framing. Generate a surface mesh for this density at isovalue 0.04 electrons/bohr³ and grid resolution 128. Wait until it is finished, then tell me the isovalue, grid resolution and triangle count. Restore the previous angle and framing after generation, check that they match the preview and show me the completed surface.

Checkpoint: a saved positive density surface now appears at 0.04 electrons/bohr³, generated on a 128³ grid. The view matches the preceding preview after the agent restores it. There is no negative-phase partner for this nonnegative total density.

A mesh is an extracted constant-density surface. It can be retained in the native file and given a surface shader; the direct sampled previews did not create this geometry.

5. See the framework through the shell

Give the density surface an X-ray finish so I can see the framework through it. Use the built-in X-ray preset with transparency enabled, zero minimum opacity, 85% maximum opacity and a cutoff that removes opacity below 3%. Keep the same viewing angle and background. Export density-xray-128.png in my output folder and show me the image.

Checkpoint: the density geometry and triangle count are unchanged, while front-facing areas become more transparent and the outline stays stronger. The shader is an appearance effect on a mesh; the density values are unchanged.

6. Cut a window into the density

Replace the X-ray finish with the built-in surface-clipping preset and make the remaining shell opaque. Keep the clipping coordinate scale at 0.24. Set the clipping box to -10 through 10 on each axis, then lower its upper z boundary to 0.25 to open a window. Rotate 35 degrees horizontally and 15 degrees vertically to inspect the opening. Export density-clipped-128.png and a transparent-background density-clipped-128-transparent.png in my output folder. Show me both.

Checkpoint: a clipping plane exposes the molecular framework inside the envelope. The saved mesh and scientific field still have the same counts and values: the shader hides fragments rather than cutting atoms or rewriting geometry. The exported transparent background is independent of the surface shader’s own transparency setting.

Keep the clipping coordinate scale fixed while moving a boundary. To look from the other side, ask the agent to rotate the view; to restore the complete shell, remove the clipping effect.

7. Save data and preserve the view

Save the current molecular document as c7h10-density.eigenvista in my output folder, using another fresh name if it exists. Confirm that the file was saved successfully and contains our latest work. Tell me what it retains. Record the current view, surface finish and appearance settings, ambient occlusion and background in your response so I can recreate this image later. Leave EigenVista running.

Checkpoint: the new molecular file exists and contains your latest work. It retains molecular data, basis/orbitals, the occupation-derived density, provenance, generated mesh and its generation settings. Global representation, surface colors, opacity and saved-mesh visibility are also document appearance.

Viewing angle, surface finish, AO and background are session settings. They are not stored in .eigenvista. Sampled orbital/density previews must also be prepared again after reopening. Keep the recorded prompts/settings and PNGs with the molecular file; reapply the appearance to recreate the view. See what is saved.

Follow progress and stop a calculation

Starting a calculation does not mean it has finished. Ask the agent to wait for the completed result before judging an image or moving to the next step. Live percentages describe the current phase, not the whole calculation. If the agent stops waiting, the app may still be working; ask it to check progress again.

If a preview or mesh step is taking longer than you want, give this prompt while that work is active:

Check the density calculation I just requested. If it is still waiting or running, cancel only that calculation and wait until the app is ready again. Keep every previously completed surface. Tell me whether it was cancelled or had already finished. If it already finished, leave its result in place.

Checkpoint: the calculation has stopped and the app is ready again. Completed geometry from earlier steps remains. If the calculation finished before cancellation, its result stays in place. Ask for a lower grid resolution if you want a quicker retry.
