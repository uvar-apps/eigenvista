07.1 / EIGENVISTA FIELD MANUAL

From a prompt to an orbital.

The current 0.10.0 source groups manual controls in four Tools tabs: Molecule, Fields, Surfaces and Appearance. You can watch or adjust the scene while your agent works; scientific requests do not depend on which tab is open. The published 0.8.0 app uses the earlier layout. See the current workspace.

Seven exchanges take a real calculation from a Molden file to a smooth, luminous orbital. Give each prompt to your external agent, watch EigenVista respond, and check the result before continuing.

Start with the connected app

Complete AI connection setup and read the external-agent guide first. These lessons use the connection tools included in the EigenVista 0.8.0 Mac app. Use your own Codex, Claude Code, Pi, OpenCode or another local agent. Type the prompts there; EigenVista has no embedded chat box.

Use the included bicyclic-c7h10.molden example, also available as a download. Your agent can download it into your input folder if needed. The input is a bicyclic C7H10 calculation: 17 atoms, 125 basis functions and 118 listed orbitals. Its original title is blank and its calculation method and basis-set name are unspecified. See the source and reuse notes.

Tell your agent which examples folder and output folder you chose during setup. Use that connected EigenVista window and those same folders throughout the lesson. Keep the original input and choose new filenames for your results.

The prompts follow the actual seven-exchange session, with portable paths and explicit checks added. Read the recorded archive or six-page PDF for the original wording and images.

1. Use your connected EigenVista window

Complete setup first, then copy this into your agent:

Use the EigenVista window we connected during setup, along with my chosen examples and output folders. Tell me what document is currently open and whether anything is unsaved. Save any unsaved work to a new .eigenvista file in my output folder before opening another document. Do not discard it or start another app. Leave EigenVista running.

Checkpoint: the agent describes the document in the window you are using. Any unsaved work is kept before you move on.

Choose fresh filenames when repeating a lesson. Existing files are not overwritten. Opening a new input is a separate action from saving your current work.

2. Ask what the file contains

Open bicyclic-c7h10.molden from my examples folder. Tell me its atom count and identify its HOMO and LUMO, including their energies and occupations. Check the complete orbital list before choosing them.

Checkpoint: the app contains 17 atoms, 125 basis functions and 118 orbitals. The agent should identify these two frontier orbitals from the supplied energies and occupations:

OrbitalSource identityEnergy / hartreeOccupation
HOMOMO 26 (26a)−0.209457810511032
LUMOMO 27 (27a)−0.0224521216828770

The first 26 listed orbitals are doubly occupied. EigenVista visualizes the supplied calculation; it does not run a new electronic-structure calculation.

3. Draw both phases of the HOMO

Remove any previous X-ray or clipping effect and use ordinary diffuse surface shading. Render the HOMO, MO 26, as positive and negative isosurfaces at an isovalue of 0.03 and grid resolution 64. Make the positive phase teal and the negative phase pink. Wait until both surfaces are finished, then fit the molecule in the view.

Checkpoint: two signed surfaces surround the framework, one at +0.03 and one at −0.03 bohr−3/2, sampled on a 64 × 64 × 64 grid. In the recorded session the positive phase is teal and the negative phase pink. Wait for generation to finish and check that both signs are visible.

This adaptation explicitly starts with ordinary surface shading when reusing an instance. Opening a document can retain a session’s X-ray or clipping shader; clearing it makes the two phase colors visible before you add the X-ray finish in step 6.

The colors show the sign of orbital amplitude, not positive and negative electric charge. Squaring an orbital removes that sign. A surface encloses a chosen amplitude level; it is not a hard boundary of the electron.

4. Compose the view

Set the background to dark blue #10243a, enable ambient occlusion for the molecule, and rotate the current view 30 degrees horizontally and 15 degrees vertically. Confirm those changes without changing the orbital surfaces.

Checkpoint: the background is dark blue, the atomic framework gains AO shading, and the camera turns from its fitted pose. The HOMO’s isovalue and mesh resolution remain unchanged.

Ambient occlusion adds visual depth to the molecule. It is a display effect, independent of the orbital calculation and the field’s two phases.

5. Export opaque and transparent PNGs

Save this view as homo.png in my output folder. Also export homo-transparent.png with a transparent background. Use fresh filenames if either exists. Show me both images here and tell me their dimensions and where they were saved.

Checkpoint: both PNG files exist and open. The opaque image includes #10243a; the transparent one has an alpha background while keeping the molecular and surface shading. The recorded exports are 1700 × 1328 pixels; your dimensions follow your actual viewport. Inspect the images, not just the filenames.

Export captures the current view and does not save a molecular document. The visible background is restored after a transparent export.

6. Give the surfaces an X-ray finish

Give both HOMO surfaces an X-ray finish: make their front-facing centers almost transparent and their contours strong and cyan. Use the built-in X-ray preset and adjust its available appearance controls. Keep the current viewing angle and dark blue background. Export a new homo-xray.png in my output folder and show it to me.

Checkpoint: the framework is visible through the shell and its contours are more prominent. Both signed surfaces remain at the same isovalue and 64³ resolution; only their finish changes.

A useful starting point is zero minimum opacity, 85% maximum opacity and a cutoff that removes opacity below 3%. These are suggested appearance settings; the recording did not preserve its exact numeric adjustments. Ask the agent to inspect the result as it tunes them. This visual finish is not an X-ray simulation.

7. Round the contours

Remember the exact current viewing angle and framing. Increase the HOMO mesh grid resolution from 64 to 128, keeping the same ±0.03 isosurfaces. Wait for the new surfaces to finish and tell me their triangle count. Restore the previous angle and framing afterward, and check that they match the preceding image. Keep the same X-ray finish and background. Export a new homo-xray-128.png in my output folder and show me the result.

Checkpoint: the surfaces have smoother contours at 128³, with the preceding view restored and the X-ray settings unchanged. The recorded final result has 18,736 triangles. Compare the settings and actual image if your result differs; a larger grid improves spatial sampling without changing the supplied wavefunction.

Regenerating geometry can change the framing automatically. Asking the agent to remember and restore the view keeps the before-and-after comparison useful.

The isovalue 0.03 specifies the magnitude for both orbital signs. Refining this orbital replaces its saved meshes. Keep a separate molecular document if you want both resolutions.

Keep exploring

To retain the scientific data and generated geometry, ask the agent to save a new .eigenvista file in your output folder. Viewing angle, shader appearance, AO and background are session settings, so keep your prompts and PNGs to reproduce the appearance. The density lesson explains what is saved and compares direct sampled rendering with mesh-based cutaways.

If a calculation is slow, use the cancellation prompt. Starting a calculation is not the same as finishing it; ask the agent to check its progress before continuing.
