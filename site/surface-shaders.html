Start with a molecular surface

Custom shaders give you direct control over how an isosurface is drawn. Load a ready-made preset to get started, or bring your own vertex and fragment GLSL files. Choose Appearance → Surface shader in the single Tools pane for the editable controls.

  1. Open bicyclic-c7h10.molden and select its total density under Fields → Quantum fields → Density matrices.
  2. In the same Fields tab, expand Field rendering. Set Isovalue to 0.04 electrons/bohr³ and Grid resolution to 256³, then click Generate surfaces. The images here use this same density mesh.
  3. Under Fields → Field rendering → Mode, select Isosurface mesh. Use Ball and stick to see the atomic framework alongside it.
  4. Frame the molecule with the rotate, zoom and Fit scene controls.

For the complete field workflow, see the orbital tutorial. The shaders below work on the generated surface, so you can try several finishes on the same geometry.

Give it an X-ray finish

This preset makes front-facing areas more transparent and keeps the outline stronger. It is useful when a surface would otherwise hide the atoms inside.

  1. Download x-ray.vert, x-ray.frag and x-ray.params.
  2. Choose Appearance and expand Surface shader. Use Vertex shader… for x-ray.vert and Fragment shader… for x-ray.frag.
  3. Use Parameters file… to load x-ray.params. Leave Shader transparency checked.
  4. Choose All surfaces under Apply to, or select an individual surface, then click Apply shader.
  5. For the illustrated finish, set maxOpacity to 0.85, minOpacity to 0 and da to 0.03. Use the toolbar’s background picker for #17232d. Rotate the molecule and adjust color to suit your view.
ControlWhat to try
colorChoose the tint of the shell.
minOpacityRaise it to give front-facing areas more body; lower it to see more of the framework.
maxOpacityLower it to soften the strongest edges.
daRaise this cutoff to remove the faintest parts of the shell and emphasize its contours.

Keep minOpacity at or below maxOpacity. The preset’s eyePosition values start at zero; leave them there for the standard view.

Cut into the surface

Clipping opens a window into a molecular envelope. Six controls bound a visible box; move one boundary across the molecule to expose what it encloses.

  1. Click Clear shader before switching presets.
  2. Load surface-clip.vert, surface-clip.frag and surface-clip.params with the same three file buttons.
  3. Choose the target under Apply to and click Apply shader.
  4. Adjust xmin and xmax, ymin and ymax, or zmin and zmax. Keep each minimum below its matching maximum. Enlarge the bounds first if the surface is mostly hidden.
  5. For the illustrated cutaway, keep factor at 0.24, set all bounds to −10 / 10, then bring zmax to 0.25. Set the surface’s Positive color to #93e8d9. Rotate the view to inspect the exposed framework.

The factor control scales the coordinates used by the clipping box. Its preset value is 0.24; leave it fixed while finding the six bounds for your molecule. For a solid finish, uncheck Shader transparency.

Tune the view live

The parameter names come directly from the shader. Numbers, checkboxes, vector components and color pickers appear as appropriate. Once a shader is applied, editing these controls updates the scene immediately: there is no need to generate the surface again.

To switch the underlying shape, choose another field or isovalue and generate its surface. To change only the appearance, keep the geometry and adjust the shader. Clear shader returns to the built-in Diffuse or Blinn-Phong surface shading.

Keep the result

  1. Use Save to keep the molecular data and generated mesh in an .eigenvista document.
  2. Click Save parameters in the shader panel to download your current settings as a .params file. Keep it with the vertex and fragment files; load all three to restore the finish in a later session.
  3. Use Export image for the current view, or Export image with transparent background to place it on a slide or figure.

The molecular document stores the surface geometry; the shader files and saved parameters keep the appearance. See saving and sharing for the document details.
